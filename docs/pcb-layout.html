  • The one placement rule that matters most. U3 (LM2596), D1 (catch diode) and L1 (inductor) form the buck's high-current switching loop — keep these three physically close with short, wide top-layer traces between them, and keep that whole cluster away from the ADC zone on the opposite side of the board. That separation is why the floorplan puts power top-left and the ADC cluster center-right, with the MCU module as a buffer between them.
  • Confirm which connector is DC input. Your schematic has two unlabeled 2-pin parts near the power stage — U1 (a 2.54mm-pitch part) and U7 ("HC-PZ200", styled like a higher-current terminal block). I've placed U7 as the 12–24V input based on its part style, but check this against your schematic before finalizing; if it's reversed, swap U1 into the power zone and move U7 elsewhere.
  • J5 is a debug header, not a sensor cable. It breaks out AVDD, AIN2, AIN3 and GND — useful for probing the local supply-check and zero-reference channels during bring-up without hunting for tiny SMD pads.
  • J3/J4/J6 pin assignments. These carry your spare GPIOs (IO0–IO7), I²C, ALERT and the HMI UART (HMI_RXD/TXD) in some combination across the three headers — the schematic's net labels didn't let me confirm the exact pin-for-pin split with certainty, so verify against your own sheet before silkscreening pin functions.
  • Rounded corners in EasyEDA. Draw the board outline on the Board Outline layer as a rectangle, then use the Fillet Corner tool (or Edit → Fillet on selected corner vertices) and enter 5mm as the radius for all four corners — simplest done after the rectangle exists, before placing keep-outs near the edges.
  • Layer usage. Top layer: component placement plus the switching loop and ADC signal traces (keep these short and direct). Bottom layer: a single unbroken ground pour plus the 5V/AVDD power traces — consistent with the no-ground-split decision from the schematic (Sheet 2 notes).
